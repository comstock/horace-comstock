Sunday, June 7 1942

Dear Folks.

I am going to drop a line that's all I'll have time for. The boys are waiting for me to go up the the Service Club with them. We'll probably play ping-pong. I was awfully glad to get you letter. It was very heartening. If that's how you say it. You asked about what to send me so here goes. The thing that's most appreciated is money because anything that we want is sold and the Post Exchange quite a lot cheaper than you could buy it. However a package from home is a very welcome sight in this army. Unparishable foods -- cookies -- candy etc. are very welcome. The things I need are slippers, Toilet set, towels, toothpaste, shaving creem, underwhere, socks (regulation). Things like these are all issued but in insuficient quantity. Unless you wash everynite. That's a lot of bother.

Incidently we had an I.Q. in Devens and I got third out of almost 300 guys. 140 out of a possible 157. That ought to help me get ny Air Cadet. The fellows are all ready to go so I'll sign off and write again tomorrow.

Loads of love, Horace
